Write-once

A meet sheet gets one clean pass, at the table, before the meet starts. Every mark after that — a DQ, a drop, a note about a rough start — gets squeezed into a margin or just remembered.

Unsearchable

Ask how your 9–10 girls did on turns this season and paper has no answer. The information exists. It's on forty sheets in a binder, if it's anywhere at all.

Gone by August

End of season, the binder goes in a box. Next year's coach starts from nothing — no history, no trend, no idea what the team actually needed to work on.

Anatomy of the deck

1

On deck

The next heat. Lane 1 to the highest lane, in an auto-sliding strip. Toggle between the whole heat and just your swimmers. Tap a card for that swimmer.

2

In the water

The heat racing now. Collapsed until you hit Start — starting expands it and arms tap-to-place. Heat nav on either side, a red End button, and it auto-advances once every lane has a time.

3

Previous

The heat that just finished. Shows heat place — full-event places wait until the whole event is done. Nothing about a finished heat is locked; it's still clickable.

Timing modes

Works with a timing system. Works without one.

Connected to a timing system

The timing system is the source of truth. Places and times come from it, and touchpad splits arrive on their own. Your job is tagging and notes — nothing to log by hand.

No timing system

Hit Start, then tap swimmers' names as they finish. Each tap assigns the next heat place. Heat place only, no times. No keyboard, no stopwatch, no typing — you can do it while watching the water.

  1. 1 Hit Start — the heat arms for tap-to-place.
  2. 2 Tap each swimmer's name as they touch the wall.
  3. 3 The heat closes itself once every lane has a place.

The tag engine

The part paper can never do.

Every tag you drop on deck rolls up automatically — team-wide, cut by age group, by stroke, and over time.
