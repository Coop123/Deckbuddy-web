On the deck

Three panels, lane 1 to the top.

The coach view splits into three stacked panels — what's coming, what's racing right now, and what just happened. Lane numbers show as the bare number. No “L.”

1

On deck

The next heat, lane 1 to the highest lane, in an auto-sliding strip. Toggle between the whole heat and just your swimmers. Tap a card for that swimmer.

2

In the water

Collapsed until you hit Start. Starting expands it and arms tap-to-place — heat nav on either side, a red End button, and it auto-advances once every lane has a time.

3

Previous

The heat that just finished. Shows heat place — full-event places wait until the whole event is done. Nothing about a finished heat is locked; it's still clickable.

The meet sheet

Event → heat → lane, kept in sync with the deck.

Toggle one event at a time or the whole meet. Live mode highlights the running heat so the sheet and the deck never disagree.

  • •Search across swimmers, events, and teams.
  • •Relays show the team plus up to four swimmers — each one individually clickable.
  • •Champs meets are 9 lanes; dual-meet lane count is selectable.

Timing

Tap-to-place now. Timing systems later.

Whenever a result carries a real time, Deck Buddy shows improvement against the seed automatically — however that time got onto the board.

Not connected Working now

Hit Start, then tap swimmers' names as they finish. Each tap assigns the next heat place. Heat place only, no times. No keyboard, no stopwatch, no typing — a coach can do it while watching the water.

Connected Planned

Reading a timing system isn't built yet. Once it is, the timing system becomes the source of truth: places and times come from it, touchpad splits arrive on their own, and the coach's job is tagging and notes.

Try it — press a chip

Tagging

Eleven chips. No tree to dig through.

A flat set of broad "needs work" chips, tapped from the swimmer popover. On deck there's no time to navigate a tree — specificity goes in the notes field.

  • •IM events prefix the tag with the leg you pick — "Back — Turns."
  • •Relay swimmers add a spot chip: Lead-off / Support / Anchor.

Splits

One tap today. Automatic with touchpads later.

A splits button opens a popup over the meet sheet — one button per home swimmer in the heat. You tap as they hit the wall. Buttons only, no typing. Once timing-system support lands, touchpad splits arrive on their own.

Scoring

Dual, champs, time trials — the math is built in.

Point values differ by meet type. Deck Buddy already knows which one you're running.

Improvement % = improved swims ÷ total swims.

Statuses

Scratch, no-show, DQ — tracked, not guessed.

All three cross the swimmer out and drop them from placing, scoring, and results.

X

Scratch

A planned withdrawal, set per-event or whole-meet from the Participants & scratches screen, for any team.

NS

No-show

Entered but didn't show. Toggled from the swimmer popover, same effect as a scratch.

DQ

Disqualification

Carries the exact USA Swimming code, for every team. Warns when you DQ a 6-and-under swimmer, and on a relay it's attributed to the specific swimmer.

Imports

Load the program once. Never retype a roster.

Hy-Tek Meet Manager program PDFs load straight in — multi-column pages get de-columned first.

  • •Hy-Tek results PDFs merge onto a loaded program by event number and name, or load as a standalone flat meet.
  • •Time Drops heat sheets come in by pasted text — it's a passcode-gated live SPA with no public feed.

Design principles

Built for wet hands and a bright deck.

Liquid glass

Translucent, layered panels that sit over content instead of boxing it in. Light blues and pinks, never a flat sheet of color.

Big tap targets

Wet hands, fast taps, moving between heats. Hitting the wrong swimmer is worse than scrolling, so targets stay big.

Legible in the sun

Every glass panel that carries text gets real blur plus a mostly-opaque ground — never body text over unblurred glass.

Light and dark, both real

Both themes ship as complete palettes, not an inverted afterthought — light blues and pinks either way.
